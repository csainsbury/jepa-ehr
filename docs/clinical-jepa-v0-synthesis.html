1. Abstract / cautious claim

Cautious claim. Clinical-JEPA v0 shows robust latent future-block retrieval on governed, re-keyed tokenised EHR sequences, including transfer to an external INSPECT validation source. The strongest external-transfer line so far is the scaled mean-token v0B scaffold; first transformer+EMA variants are strong on MIMIC and show real INSPECT signal, but do not yet beat the simpler scaled mean-token scaffold under the demanding MIMIC→INSPECT zero-shot transfer gate.
Prior-art boundary. Yang et al. 2026 Clin-JEPA is direct prior art for generic JEPA-style latent rollout over EHR patient trajectories. This report should not be read as claiming first-EHR-JEPA novelty; the local differentiator is governed tokenised-EHR readout, FlatASCEND/ORCA reader-speaker bridging, INSPECT transfer, leakage controls, and TTE-style generation/readout readiness.

The v0 programme tested whether a JEPA-style latent prediction objective can learn useful clinical state-transition representations from tokenised EHR sequences, without collapsing into a FlatASCEND/autoregressive teacher-copy story. The answer is provisionally yes: the signal survives leakage checks, matched distractors, horizon stress, candidate-normalized retrieval, row-shuffle controls, query-shuffle controls, within-group time-shift controls, and source-transfer evaluation.

The result is not a clinical outcome model and not a causal treatment-effect claim. It is evidence that latent future-state prediction over clinical event streams is a viable representation-learning objective worth developing further.

4. Main results

4.1 Evidence chain

  1. The minimal v0B JEPA scaffold learned non-collapsed real-data representations.
  2. v0B target retrieval beat frozen FlatASCEND v0A target-prediction baselines under multiple retrieval framings.
  3. Retrieval degraded monotonically with larger event gaps, consistent with a horizon-sensitive predictor.
  4. Length, sequence-length, utilisation/context-count matching, candidate normalization, and row/query/time controls did not explain the main signal.
  5. MIMIC-trained v0B showed external INSPECT transfer; MIMIC-trained v0A transfer was much weaker.
  6. Scaling the mean-token v0B scaffold improved retrieval strongly, with a MIMIC-vs-transfer trade-off.
  7. Transformer+EMA variants improved MIMIC retrieval and transferred clearly above controls, but did not yet beat scaled mean-token models under the demanding MIMIC→INSPECT zero-shot transfer gate.

5. Final comparison

The transfer robustness gate is candidate-normalized retrieval on INSPECT, especially gap16/gap64. MIMIC gap0 is useful but not decisive because source-specialised representations can look strong there.

6. Interpretation and limitations

What this supports

  • Latent future-block prediction is a viable clinical representation-learning objective.
  • The signal is not explained away by the current leakage, length/utilisation, candidate-size, or shuffle/time controls.
  • External-source transfer is present, especially for scaled mean-token variants.

What it does not support yet

  • No clinical outcome utility claim.
  • No causal treatment-effect claim.
  • No claim that transformer+EMA is the best architecture.
  • No claim that retrieval equals clinical interpretability.

Working interpretation

The simplest interpretation is that v0B learns a real latent transition signal, but richer sequence modelling may over-specialise to source-specific token geometry unless regularised or trained/evaluated with source robustness in mind. The current best transfer behaviour comes from scaled mean-token variants, which may act as a useful regulariser by discarding some source-specific sequence detail.

9. Next experiment brief — generation and TTE readiness first

Correction to the transfer-optimisation instinct: the next conceptual bottleneck is not immediately improving MIMIC→INSPECT transfer. The core question is whether JEPA-style latent state prediction can help produce clinically reasonable explicit futures. We have not yet tested that.

How far we got technically

  • Completed: governed token substrate, prefix-safe target blocks, leakage audits, v0A baselines, v0B mean-token JEPA, transformer+EMA diagnostics, matched retrieval, candidate-normalized retrieval, controls, and INSPECT zero-shot evaluation.
  • Not completed: no JEPA decoder/renderer, no JEPA-conditioned autoregressive rollout, no clinical plausibility evaluation of JEPA-generated sequences, and no TTE-style audit of generated futures.
  • Current bridge: the retrieval setup can serve as a pseudo-renderer by retrieving observed future blocks nearest to the predicted latent future state. That is not generation, but it is a good first test of whether latent predictions select clinically plausible futures.

Next atom A: TTE/specification data-quality audit

Before training another model, define one medication-initiation or active-comparator scenario as a target-trial-style specification card. This is a data/specification readiness test, not a causal estimate.

Specification fieldsEligibility, incident-user definition, active comparator/equivalent contact, time zero, baseline lookback, follow-up, outcome/proxy candidates, censoring, negative controls, surveillance/contact-intensity controls, leakage exclusions.
Aggregate outputsCohort counts, incident initiators, comparator candidates, baseline completeness, follow-up availability, event rates, observation density, exclusion reasons, MIMIC vs INSPECT source differences, and bias/leakage warnings.
Pass gateAt least one scenario has enough MIMIC subjects and is measurable in INSPECT, with prefix-safe definitions and usable negative/surveillance controls.

Next atom B: retrieval-based pseudo-rendering smoke test

If the TTE/specification audit passes, use existing JEPA embeddings without new training: predict latent future state, retrieve top-k matched observed target blocks, and assess whether those retrieved futures are clinically/specification-consistent in aggregate.

ChecksEvent-family distribution, medication/lab/state density, horizon/time-gap plausibility, TTE consistency, treatment-strategy consistency, impossible transition rates, negative-control rates, and surveillance artifacts.
SuccessRetrieved futures are much more specification-consistent than shuffle controls and reveal plausible future-state structure rather than just utilisation/contact-density matching.
StopStop before new model training if no clean TTE-style scenario exists, outcome/proxy labels are not prefix-safe, or pseudo-rendering is mostly utilisation/contact-density.

Only later: explicit renderer/rollout bridge

After the above passes, choose between a retrieval renderer, a FlatASCEND speaker bridge, or a small JEPA decoder head. Transfer-aware transformer+EMA regularisation remains a later experiment, downstream of the generation/readout question.
